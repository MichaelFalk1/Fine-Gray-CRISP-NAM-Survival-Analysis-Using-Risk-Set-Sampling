\begin{abstract}
Survival analysis under competing risks presents significant computational and methodological challenges, particularly when modelling large-scale data with complex dependency structures. This study investigates the impact of risk set sampling strategies within the Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard framework, combined with interpretable deep learning models such as CRISP-NAM. We introduce a novel entropy-guided grouped sampling approach that dynamically approximates risk sets, aiming to balance computational efficiency with statistical accuracy. The proposed method is integrated into a Deep Fine-Gray modelling framework and evaluated against full-risk set approaches. Empirical results demonstrate that the proposed sampling strategies substantially reduce training time and computational resource requirements, while maintaining comparable predictive performance and statistical validity. This research contributes to Sustainable Development Goal~3 (Good Health and Well-being) by improving scalable and interpretable survival modelling techniques that can support better health risk prediction and decision-making in resource-constrained settings.
\end{abstract}
